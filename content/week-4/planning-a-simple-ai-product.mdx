# Planning a Simple AI Product

> A simple product plan turns a good idea into something clear enough to build, test, and explain.

## Lesson Goal

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to describe a basic AI product plan.

You will connect:

- the user
- the problem
- the AI feature
- the input
- the output
- the first version
- the success criteria

This is the bridge between an idea and a project.

---

## Start With The User

A product plan begins with the user.

Ask:

- Who is this for?
- What do they already do?
- Where do they get stuck?
- What result do they want?

Do not write "everyone."

Choose one clear user group.

For example:

> First-year students who need to plan study time before exams.

That is easier to design for than "students."

---

## Define The Problem

The problem should be specific.

Weak problem:

> Studying is hard.

Stronger problem:

> Students often do not know how to divide a topic into small study sessions before an exam.

The stronger version tells you what the product needs to help with.

---

## Define The AI Feature

Your AI feature should connect directly to the problem.

If the problem is planning study time, the feature might be:

> AI turns a topic, deadline, and available time into a simple study plan.

This is focused.

The AI has a clear job.

---

## Input

Input is what the user gives the product.

For an AI study planner, the input could be:

- topic
- exam date
- current confidence level
- available days
- time available per day

Good input makes better output possible.

If the input is too vague, the output will usually be vague too.

---

## Output

Output is what the product gives back.

For an AI study planner, the output could be:

- a day-by-day study plan
- a list of topics to review
- practice question suggestions
- a final revision checklist

The output should be easy to use.

The user should know what to do next.

---

## First Version / MVP

MVP means minimum viable product.

In simple words:

> The smallest useful version of the product.

Your first version should not include every possible feature.

It should prove the main idea.

For the AI study planner, the first version might only ask for:

- topic
- exam date
- available study time

Then it returns a simple plan.

No accounts. No calendar sync. No advanced analytics.

Just the core value.

---

## Success Criteria

Success criteria help you know whether the product is useful.

Ask:

- Did the user get a clear output?
- Did the output save time?
- Did the output reduce confusion?
- Would the user use it again?
- Did the product solve the original problem?

Success does not have to mean millions of users.

For a beginner project, success can mean:

> A user can create a useful study plan in under two minutes.

---

## Example: AI Study Planner

User:

Students preparing for exams.

Problem:

They do not know how to break a large topic into manageable study sessions.

AI feature:

AI creates a simple study plan from the topic, deadline, and available time.

Input:

- topic
- exam date
- study time per day

Output:

- daily study plan
- review checklist
- practice question suggestions

First version:

A single-page form that returns a 7-day study plan.

Success criteria:

The user gets a plan they understand and can follow today.

## Mini Exercise

Create your own simple AI product plan.

Write:

1. User
2. Problem
3. AI feature
4. Input
5. Output
6. First version
7. Success criteria

Keep each answer short.

If it becomes complicated, make the first version smaller.
