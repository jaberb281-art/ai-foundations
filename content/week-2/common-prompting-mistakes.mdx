# Common Prompting Mistakes

> Most bad AI answers start with unclear instructions, missing context, or too much trust in the first response.

## Lesson Goal

By the end of this lesson, you will know the most common beginner prompting mistakes and how to fix them.

Prompting is a skill. You will get better by noticing what went wrong and improving the instruction.

---

## Mistake 1: Being Too Vague

Vague prompts create vague answers.

Weak prompt:

> Give me ideas.

The AI does not know what kind of ideas you want.

Better prompt:

> Give me 10 beginner-friendly project ideas for learning AI with Python. Keep them simple, practical, and possible to finish in one week.

The better prompt gives the AI a clear direction.

---

## Mistake 2: Giving No Context

AI does not automatically know your situation.

Weak prompt:

> Make this better.

Better prompt:

> I am writing a course description for beginner developers who want to learn AI. Rewrite this to sound clear, practical, and not too salesy.

Context helps the AI understand the audience and purpose.

Without context, the answer may be technically fine but not useful for your real situation.

---

## Mistake 3: Asking For Too Many Things At Once

AI can handle complex tasks, but beginners often overload a prompt.

Weak prompt:

> Create a full course, write all lessons, make quizzes, build a project, design the website, and create a launch plan.

That is too much for one useful answer.

Better approach:

Start with one focused task:

> Create a five-week outline for a beginner AI course. For each week, give the topic, learning goal, and one project idea.

Then ask follow-up questions.

Good prompting is often a conversation, not one giant request.

---

## Mistake 4: Not Checking AI Output

AI can sound confident and still be wrong.

This matters for:

- facts
- code
- legal information
- medical information
- financial advice
- personal decisions

Do not treat the first answer as final.

Ask:

> What assumptions are you making?

Ask:

> What could be wrong with this answer?

Ask:

> Give me sources or tell me what I should verify.

Checking the output is part of using AI well.

---

## Mistake 5: Treating AI As Always Correct

AI is a tool, not an authority.

It can help you think, draft, compare, summarize, and practice.

But it does not replace your judgment.

If the answer affects real people, money, health, safety, or reputation, slow down and verify it.

The best AI users stay curious and skeptical at the same time.

## Simple Correction Examples

### Too vague

Weak:

> Explain prompts.

Better:

> Explain prompts to a beginner who has used ChatGPT but does not understand why some answers are better than others. Use simple examples.

### No output format

Weak:

> Help me plan my day.

Better:

> Create a daily plan in a table with three columns: Time, Task, and Why it matters.

### No constraints

Weak:

> Write a bio.

Better:

> Write a professional bio under 80 words for a beginner AI educator. Keep the tone warm, clear, and credible.

---

## A Simple Fixing Process

When an AI answer is weak, do not immediately blame the tool.

First ask:

1. Was my task clear?
2. Did I give enough context?
3. Did I define the output format?
4. Did I give constraints?
5. Did I ask the AI to explain or improve its answer?

Then revise the prompt and try again.

That loop is how you improve.
