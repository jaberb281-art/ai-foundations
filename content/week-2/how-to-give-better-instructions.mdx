# How to Give Better Instructions

> Better prompts usually come from better instructions, not longer instructions.

## Lesson Goal

By the end of this lesson, you will know a simple structure for writing better prompts:

- role
- context
- task
- constraints
- output format

You do not need to use every part every time. But when an AI answer feels weak, these parts help you improve it.

---

## Role

The role tells the AI what kind of helper it should act like.

Examples:

- You are a patient tutor.
- You are a career coach.
- You are a code reviewer.
- You are a beginner-friendly AI teacher.

Role matters because it changes the style and focus of the answer.

For example:

> Explain this as a senior engineer.

will usually feel different from:

> Explain this as a patient tutor for a beginner.

---

## Context

Context tells the AI the situation.

Without context, the AI has to guess.

Useful context might include:

- your skill level
- your goal
- your audience
- what you have already tried
- what problem you are solving

Example:

> I am a beginner learning AI. I understand basic Python, but I do not know machine learning yet.

This helps the AI choose the right level.

---

## Task

The task tells the AI exactly what to do.

Examples:

- Explain this concept.
- Turn these notes into a checklist.
- Create five practice questions.
- Rewrite this paragraph for beginners.
- Compare these two options.

A clear task prevents the AI from drifting.

If you ask:

> Help me with this.

the AI has to guess what help means.

If you ask:

> Summarize this into five bullet points for a beginner.

the task is clear.

---

## Constraints

Constraints are limits or rules.

They tell the AI what to avoid or respect.

Examples:

- Keep it under 150 words.
- Avoid technical jargon.
- Use simple examples.
- Do not make assumptions.
- Focus only on beginner concepts.

Constraints are useful when the answer is too long, too advanced, too vague, or too broad.

---

## Output Format

Output format tells the AI how to present the answer.

Examples:

- Use a table.
- Use bullet points.
- Give a step-by-step plan.
- End with three quiz questions.
- Use this format: Problem, Cause, Fix.

Format matters because a good answer can still be hard to use if it is not organized.

---

## Before And After Prompt Example

Weak prompt:

> Help me study AI.

Better prompt:

> You are a patient AI tutor. I am a beginner learning AI Foundations. Create a 30-minute study plan for understanding how AI learns from data. Keep it practical, use simple language, and include a short quiz at the end.

The better prompt includes:

- role: patient AI tutor
- context: beginner learning AI Foundations
- task: create a 30-minute study plan
- constraints: practical and simple language
- output format: include a short quiz

This gives the AI a much clearer target.

---

## Mini Exercise: Improve A Weak Prompt

Weak prompt:

> Write a caption.

Improve it by adding:

- role
- context
- task
- constraints
- output format

Example improved version:

> You are a social media copywriter. I am posting about a beginner AI course for developers. Write five Instagram captions that sound clear, helpful, and not hype-driven. Keep each caption under 140 characters.

Now the AI has enough detail to produce something useful.

---

## Practical Prompt Template

Use this structure when you are stuck:

```text
Role:
You are a [kind of assistant].

Context:
I am trying to [goal]. My audience is [audience]. My constraints are [constraints].

Task:
Create [output] that helps me achieve [result].

Format:
Use [format].
```

You can change the labels, but the thinking pattern stays useful.
