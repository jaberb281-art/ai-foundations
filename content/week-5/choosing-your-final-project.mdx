# Choosing Your Final Project

> A strong final project is not the biggest idea. It is the clearest useful idea you can explain and finish.

## Lesson Goal

By the end of this lesson, you will choose a final AI project that is useful, realistic, and portfolio-worthy.

Your final project should show what you learned across the course.

It should also be small enough to complete.

---

## What Makes A Good Final Project

A good final project has four qualities.

It solves a real problem.

It is for a clear user.

It uses AI for a specific task.

It can be explained simply.

You do not need to build a huge app.

You need a clear project that shows your thinking.

---

## Choose A Real Problem

Start with the problem, not the tool.

Ask:

- Who needs help?
- What are they trying to do?
- Where do they get stuck?
- Why does the problem matter?

Example:

> Students applying for internships struggle to write strong resume bullet points.

That is better than:

> I want to build an AI resume thing.

The first version gives you something real to solve.

---

## Choose The Right Scope

Scope means how big the project is.

A good beginner scope is small and clear.

Too broad:

> An AI career platform that writes resumes, finds jobs, prepares interviews, and tracks applications.

Better:

> An AI helper that turns rough student experience notes into three improved resume bullet options.

The smaller version is easier to build, test, and explain.

---

## Avoid Projects That Are Too Big

Your final project should not require:

- a full company
- many user roles
- complex payments
- a huge database
- every possible feature
- perfect automation

Those things can come later.

For now, choose a project that proves one useful idea.

---

## Reuse Work From Previous Weeks

You do not need to start from zero.

You can reuse:

- your Week 1 study assistant prompt
- your Week 2 prompt pack
- your Week 3 content workflow
- your Week 4 mini product plan

Your final project can be an improved version of something you already started.

That is normal.

Real projects often grow from earlier drafts.

---

## Final Project Options

Choose one of these paths.

### AI study assistant

Create a reusable assistant that explains a topic, quizzes the learner, reviews answers, and suggests next steps.

### Prompt pack

Create a polished set of prompts for one real use case.

### AI content workflow

Show a repeatable workflow that turns one idea into a useful content draft.

### AI mini product plan

Turn a real problem into a simple product concept with input, output, first version, and success criteria.

### Custom AI project

Choose your own project if it has a clear user, problem, AI role, and final output.

---

## Mini Exercise

Pick one final project option.

Write:

1. Project title
2. User
3. Problem
4. AI role
5. Final output
6. Why this project is realistic

Keep each answer short.

If the project feels too big, make the first version smaller.

---

## Reflection Question

What final project can you finish, explain clearly, and proudly show as evidence of what you learned?
