# Building the Project Story

> A project is easier to trust when people can understand the problem, the process, and the result.

## Lesson Goal

By the end of this lesson, you will know how to explain your AI project as a simple case study.

A case study is the story of what you built and why it matters.

It does not need to be long.

It needs to be clear.

---

## Why Project Story Matters

People should not have to guess what your project does.

A good project story helps them understand:

- the problem you noticed
- who the project is for
- how you used AI
- what you created
- what improved along the way
- what the final result shows

This matters for portfolios, applications, interviews, and personal learning.

The story proves your thinking.

---

## Start With The Problem

Begin with the problem in plain language.

Weak:

> I made an AI tool.

Stronger:

> Students applying for internships often struggle to turn basic experience into clear resume bullet points.

The stronger version gives the project a reason to exist.

---

## Name The User

Say who the project is for.

Examples:

- first-year students
- beginner freelancers
- small business owners
- content creators
- job seekers

The user helps people understand your design choices.

If the user is vague, the project will feel vague too.

---

## Explain The Process

Your process is how you moved from idea to output.

You might describe:

- how you chose the problem
- what information you collected
- what prompts you tested
- what workflow you used
- what you changed after reviewing the AI output

Do not only show the final answer.

Show how you got there.

---

## Explain The AI Role

Be specific about what AI helped with.

AI might have helped you:

- brainstorm options
- rewrite text
- create an outline
- summarize information
- compare ideas
- generate a first draft
- improve clarity

Also explain what AI did not decide.

Your judgment still matters.

---

## Show The Output

The output is what your project produced.

Examples:

- a study assistant prompt
- a prompt pack
- a content workflow
- a product plan
- a final case study

Make the output easy to understand.

If possible, include a short example later.

---

## Describe The Result

The result explains why the project is useful.

Ask:

- What became clearer?
- What became faster?
- What became easier?
- What can the user do now?

You do not need to exaggerate.

A simple useful result is enough.

---

## What Changed From First Idea To Final Version

Good projects improve.

You can mention:

- what was too broad at first
- what you simplified
- what prompt became better
- what output you manually improved
- what limitation you noticed

This shows learning.

It also makes your project feel honest.

---

## Example Case Study Outline

Use this structure:

1. Project title
2. Problem
3. User
4. AI role
5. Process
6. Output
7. Result
8. What I improved
9. What I would build next

You can write one short paragraph for each section.

Keep it clear before you make it fancy.

---

## Mini Exercise

Write a rough case study outline for your final project.

Use these headings:

- Problem
- User
- Process
- AI role
- Output
- Result
- What changed

Do not worry about perfect wording yet.

---

## Reflection Question

What part of your project story best shows your thinking, not just the AI output?
